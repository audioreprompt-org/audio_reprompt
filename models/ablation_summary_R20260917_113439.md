# Reprompt Ablation Summary (Run: `R20260917_113439`)

This document aggregates the comprehensive evaluation of the four prompt versions (V1–V4) used in our music descriptor pipeline. We analyzed their performance via CLAP audio-text alignment, tracked textual distance metrics to rule out semantic drift artifacts, and used an LLM judge to evaluate the quality of the generated musical instructions.

**Dataset Note:** All results below (CLAP Scores, Text Distance, and LLM Judge metrics) are based on a **stratified sample of 40 prompts per version** (10 for each `taste` category: sweet, sour, salty, bitter) to ensure a fair and balanced evaluation across variants.

---

## 0. CLAP Score Results & t-tests

The primary goal of the reprompts is to increase the text-to-audio alignment (CLAP Score) compared to the raw prompts. We evaluated the 4 versions (B1a-B1d ablations) to see which template generated audio that best matched its text representation. 

* **V1 (Melody)**: Δ μ = +0.0575, p-value = **0.0375 \***, Cohen's d = **0.4292**
* **V2 (Harmony)**: Δ μ = +0.0400, p-value = 0.1490 (n.s.), Cohen's d = 0.3110
* **V3 (Rhythm)**: Δ μ = +0.1173, p-value = **0.0005 \*\*\***, Cohen's d = **0.8817**
* **V4 (All)**: Δ μ = +0.0070, p-value = 0.8071 (n.s.), Cohen's d = 0.0478

> [!TIP]
> **V3 (Rhythm-focused)** was decisively the best performer. It yielded the largest average improvement in CLAP score (+107.6% boost over the raw prompt baseline) with high statistical significance (p=0.0005) and a large effect size (d=0.88).

## 1. Text Distance Analysis

**Why we did it:**  
We wanted to ensure that the massive CLAP score jump for V3 wasn't just a byproduct of its text representation drifting further away from the raw prompt than the other versions. If V3 simply added more "noise" or strayed further in the text embedding space, its apparent CLAP success could be an artificial artifact of the text encoder rather than a genuine improvement in audio alignment.

**Results:**  
Using the pure semantic `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` encoder, we measured the cosine distance between the raw prompt and the generated reprompts:
- **V1**: 0.8019
- **V2**: 0.8059
- **V3**: 0.8055
- **V4**: 0.7974

**Conclusion:**  
The semantic "drift" introduced when generating the reprompts is virtually identical across all four ablation versions. Because the textual distance is equal everywhere, V3's downstream differences in CLAP score cannot be blamed on the reprompt just straying too far from the raw text. The gap is driven by the *specific, taste-relevant vocabulary and structural qualities* that V3 injects into the prompt.

## 2. LLM Judge Rubric Evaluation

**The Idea:**  
To further understand the *quality* of the text instructions generated by the 4 prompts, we built an LLM judge script (`evaluate_reprompts_llm_judge.py`) to systematically score the reprompts against a rigorous rubric of baseline music features *(pitch, contour, range, tesitura, phrasing, interval, root, consonance, progression, tonality, beat, pulse, tempo, meter, accent)*.

The judge evaluated 3 dimensions:
1. **Cohesion (0-5)**: Correctness, alignment, and detail of the musical descriptors.
2. **Actionability (1-5)**: Clarity of instructions for an audio-generation model.
3. **Coherence (1-5)**: Congruence of how the descriptors define melody, harmony, and rhythm.

**Judge System Prompt:**
```text
You are an expert music curator and evaluator.
Your task is to evaluate a music generation reprompt text using the following baseline music features:
- pitch, contour, range, tesitura, phrasing, interval, root, consonance, progression, tonality, beat, pulse, tempo, meter, accent

You must evaluate the reprompt using the following three metrics and output your evaluation as a strict JSON object:

1. "Cohesion Text Level": Score 0 to 5 for the correctness of the existing music descriptors. Use 1-5 to indicate how much the descriptor is aligned in the entire text and its level of detail. Put 0 if there is no descriptor related to the baseline music descriptors.
2. "Actionability Level": Score 1 to 5 for the clarity and actionability of the existing music descriptors for an audio-generation model.
3. "Coherence Music Level": Score 1 to 5 for how congruent the music descriptors define a melody, harmony, and rhythm.

Output format:
{
  "Cohesion Text Level": <int>,
  "Actionability Level": <int>,
  "Coherence Music Level": <int>
}
```

**Full Dataset Results (`gpt-5.6-luna`, n=160):**

| Version | Cohesion (0-5) | Actionability (1-5) | Coherence (1-5) | Overall (Avg) |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **V1**  | 4.97           | 4.53                | 4.62            | **4.71**      |
| **V2**  | 4.92           | 4.58                | 4.58            | **4.69**      |
| **V3**  | 4.90           | 4.58                | 4.58            | **4.68**      |
| **V4**  | 4.82           | 4.40                | 4.53            | **4.58**      |

**Conclusion:**  
All versions generate incredibly cohesive and coherent musical instructions according to the LLM judge. The differences in "perceived text quality" are negligible (all scoring ~4.7/5). This reinforces that V3's superior CLAP scores aren't simply because it writes "better sounding text", but because focusing the generation model specifically on *rhythm* features (as opposed to melody or harmony) creates a significantly stronger text-audio alignment for these taste-based crossmodal prompts.
